Spent two days at NEXTPredict in New York listening to exchanges, market makers, banks and regulators describe the prediction-market product's problems. I kept writing down the same one.

A position on a prediction market can only be won or lost. That is why half the new users are gone by month three. It is why the hedge funds get a bespoke hedge from a market maker and the person holding $200 of Colts gets nothing. It is why everyone is nervous about $18bn a week of parlay risk. And it is why "trader protection" still means a deposit limit nobody sets.

We built the other thing.

You hold a position. You tap once. You get a floor if it loses, and you get paid a credit right now for taking a bit less if it wins. Your position stays where it is, in your account, on your venue. We go build the hedge ourselves on a regulated exchange, in our own account, and write every piece of it to a public record, down to the exchange's order id. If the books can't fund an honest credit, you get told that instead of a bad quote.

It is live on a perpetuals venue today and running on a public record across event-contract venues. The parlay version is next.

## Paste procedure for Medium

1. Open `nextpredict_2026_paper_medium.html` in a browser.
2. Select all, copy.
3. In Medium, New story, paste into the body. Headings, italics, bold, blockquote-free inline quotes, lists and links carry across. The horizontal rules carry as Medium section breaks.
4. Medium takes the first H1 as the title and the first H2 as the subtitle. Confirm both, then delete the duplicate byline line if Medium has already attached your profile as author.
5. Add a cover image (a photo from the summit floor or the Atticus wordmark on a plain field). Without one, the LinkedIn preview card is text-only.
6. Publish settings: add the five tags, keep "Allow curators to recommend" on, publish under the Atticus publication if one exists, otherwise under the founder profile.
7. After publishing, copy the story URL into the LinkedIn post above and into the first comment.

## Before publishing

- The 1.89% against 3.15% hedge-cost comparison is read from the current shadow. Re-check the number on the day of posting and update the paper if it has moved.
- "Live on a perpetuals venue" and "running on a public record across event-contract venues" describe deployment on the date of writing. Keep them true or soften them.
- Speakers are named with the title and session given in the source. If any speaker asks not to be quoted, remove the name and leave the session; the argument does not depend on the names.
