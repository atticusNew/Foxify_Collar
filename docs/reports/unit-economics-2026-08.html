1. The Product in Four Sentences

A Hyperliquid trader taps one toggle. The options market pays them a daily credit and puts a hard floor under their position. Their funds never move: no deposit, no keys, nothing to learn. Toggle on: credit plus floor. Toggle off: neither. Everything else is identical.

It is the collar strategy institutional desks run every day, executed in one tap. We place both option legs on a listed venue from our own sub-account; the difference between the sold cap and the bought floor is the trader's credit.

3. What the Collateral Does

Collateral is not spend and not trading capital. It sits in an OKX sub-account as options margin, verifiable by independent read-only API access. Margin is consumed while a wrap is open and returns at the daily close, so the same capital backs a book that recycles every day.

Credits grow with four levers: position size (always linear), execution tier (block RFQ pricing measured 2 to 5 times better than retail fills), volume density (long and short wraps net internally, so our hedge orders reach block size sooner), and volatility (busy days pay multiples of quiet ones; we measure it, we do not promise it).

5. What Protects the Collateral

LayerPlain reading
Withdrawal-disabled API keysKeys are trade-only at the venue level. Even a total server compromise cannot move funds off the exchange.
Independent visibilityThe capital partner holds their own read-only API access to the sub-account at all times.
Atomic pairsBoth option legs fill or the pair auto-unwinds. A fill worse than the quoted credit auto-unwinds. Enforced in code, proven on real orders.
Permanent buffer40% of capital is never allocated. Utilization auto-pauses new wraps before the buffer is touched. A kill switch stops new exposure while payouts keep running.
Concentration capsBook, per-wallet, and per-strike caps bound exposure. The payout wallet is separate, small, and per-day capped: its float is the maximum blast radius.
Full reconciliationEvery wrap, fill, and payout is recorded and reconciled against the venue's own records. A public dashboard shows the live book.
