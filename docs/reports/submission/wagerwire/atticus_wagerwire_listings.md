Each ticket is a parlay whose hit legs are done and whose last leg is a future still running. That makes it one position on one market, which is the shape we protect. The lock is a firm floor and cap on that leg, hedged leg for leg on Polymarket, with a credit paid the moment the seller taps.

Pays: the ticket's full payout if the last leg hits, as listed, with the month it resolves. Fair now: payout times the venue's live price on that leg. Lock, worst: the floor on every dollar of payout plus the credit; the seller has at least this whatever happens. Lock, if it hits: the cap plus the credit. Paid now: the credit at the tap.

## What this is

- The lock is a collar on the remaining leg: the seller sells the payout above the cap and buys a floor under it, in one tap, and is paid the difference now. We hedge every contract on the venue's book at the same moment, so we carry no outcome risk; we earn the stated take plus the stated carry, whoever wins.
- Fair is the venue's price, not ours. Where the ask sits above fair, no buyer will come; where it sits below, the seller is giving value away. The lock's worst case is a number with a counterparty behind it.
- A future resolves months out and the hedge sits until then, so the credit carries a time charge, shown on every row. The live book for games settling within days has none.
- Every number here comes from a public page read by a person and a public order book read at the time stamped above. Prices move; the morning of a conversation we rerun the page.

When the legs resolve we mark every row against the official result: what the lock paid, what the ask would have paid, what the ticket did naked. The follow-up writes itself. Michael Ting, michael@atticustrade.com.
